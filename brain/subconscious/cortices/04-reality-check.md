## When

The turn rests on a claim about the outside world, or on a decision that depends on one, where current evidence could change the answer: a number, a market, a law, a health or money fact, what usually happens to people who try this, or anything that may have changed lately. It matters most when the answer just given took the claim on trust.

Not for opinions and taste, things only the person can know, facts that do not change, or a live lookup they asked for (that is a job, and a Worker already has it).

## Then

Check the decisive claim against the outside world. Go to primary and trusted sources, and look for what usually happens in cases like this one. Weigh what supports the claim and what contradicts it; judge the quality of a source, not the number of sources. When it turns on numbers, give the denominator, the time frame and the range.

Hand back a short brief: what you verified, with links and dates; the best-supported conclusion (supported, refuted, mixed, or cannot tell); how sure; what is still unknown.

If the evidence supports what was said, say so plainly: do not manufacture doubt to look rigorous. Never invent a source or a result. If you could not check, say that you could not, and why.
