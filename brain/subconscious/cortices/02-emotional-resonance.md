## When

The emotional meaning of what was just said is central, or indirect cues could change how it should be understood: words and tone that do not match, guarded or masking language, mixed feelings, tension between people, a real shift in how they write or speak, or a direct question about what someone may be feeling or leaving unsaid.

Not for frustration at a task they simply want done, routine thanks or excitement, or a request that fixes the shape of the answer (one step, no lecture).

## Then

Read the emotional room. From the evidence in the conversation only (word choice, phrasing, pace, what is left out, a change of style), say what the person, or the person they describe, may be feeling and not saying.

Name the strongest reading and the exact cue behind it, then what the conscious mind should take into account because of it. Any feeling counts: joy, pride, attraction, anger, fear, grief, shame, boredom, numbness. Do not default to distress or to comfort.

Keep what is observed apart from what is inferred, and never claim to see a face or hear a tone that was not given. If the evidence supports no reading that matters, hand back nothing.
